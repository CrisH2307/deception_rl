### 1. Arm A finding to confirm: the three optima coincide together

Measured over the full 200,000-candidate pool by `src/k2_gate.py`
(`reports/T2_k2_gate.md`), and reproduced on P1's frozen 1,000-item set by
`src/frozen_set_beta_c.py` (`reports/T2_frozen_set_counts.md`):

| conditioned on | N | share adversary-robust (`beta_c = infinity`) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `fit_cost = 0` (non-conflict) | 162,694 | 98.29% |
| `fit_cost > 0` (conflict) | 37,306 | 9.71% |

On the frozen set the same relation reads 0.0160 against 0.9040 in the other
direction: of its 460 finite-`beta_c` items, 452 are conflict items.

**Why this is substantive rather than a nuisance.** The two conditions are different
statements about different pairs of optima:

- `fit_cost = 0` says the **salience** optimum and the **Bayes** optimum coincide,
  `o_fit = o_bayes`.
- `beta_c = infinity` says the **Bayes** optimum and the **margin** optimum coincide,
  `o*_0 = argmax_o margin(o)`.

They share one term and are otherwise unrelated: neither implies the other, and a
signal space in which they were independent would show, in both rows, a robust rate
near the pooled marginal of 0.8177 (the complement of the 0.1823 existence rate).
Instead the rows differ by 88.6 points. The three optima
coincide together far more often than chance, and the adversary effect concentrates
almost entirely inside P1's conflict set: outside it, the optimal signal is already
margin-maximizing and no persuasion budget moves it.

**What T6 does with this.** Confirm it on Arm A's own item base and report it with
the `beta_c` characterization in Step 2. Step 4 is the natural home for the mechanism
question, and remains exploratory there, as spec v3.1 section 7.2 already assigns it.
Do not treat the coincidence as established because it is written here: it was
measured as a byproduct of a gate computation, on a pool, by a session whose brief
was item design.
