# T6: Arm A. The K1 kill gate and the `beta_c` characterisation

Generated by `python3 src/t6_arm_a.py` on 2026-09-09. Deterministic, no seed enters any quantity. Spec `v3.1`. No language model was run.

**Item bases, which this script did not choose.** Step 1 runs on the full unstratified 200,000-candidate pool (v2.0 section 2, section 7.1). Steps 2 through 5 run on P1's frozen `items_final.parquet`, all 1,000 items, all four tiles: v2.1 section 1.3 fixes Arm A's base as "T2's frozen item set" and v2.2 section 1.1 fixes what that resolves to now that K2 has fired. Restriction to the `size` tile is Arm B's (v2.3 section 3) and does not reach Arm A. Where a pool figure is cheap because Step 1 computes it anyway it is reported beside the frozen-set figure and labelled.

**Which base a rate claim uses.** The pool rate is a property of the signal space. The frozen rate is a property of P1's 50/50 conflict quota. Any claim of the form "X% of items diverge" therefore uses the **pool** figure: **0.1823** existence, **0.1795** at the grid endpoint. The frozen set's 0.4570 at the endpoint and 0.4600 existence are reported only as the Arm A base's own rates, never as population statements. This applies to the divergence rate, the existence rate, the grid rate, the per-tile rates, and the share of items above the grid endpoint. It is a labelling rule, not a recomputation, and it is emitted to `results/T6_arm_a_numbers.json` under `rate_claim_rule` so a downstream task does not have to re-derive it.

## Primary findings

1. **The kill gate passes.** `|D(infinity)| / N` = 0.1823 on the 200,000-candidate pool against a threshold of 0.10. The adversary effect exists in this signal space and the reporting grid reaches it. Arms B and C may run.

2. **Robustness against an informed adversary requires abandoning Bayesian discrimination entirely and reverting to the salient signal.** `o*_infinity` is P1's `o_fit` on **452 of 460** divergent items in the frozen set (0.9826) and on 0.9246 of the pool's 36,464. Measured on P1's own frontier coordinate the price of robustness is exactly the whole salience-to-Bayes interval, on every item where that coordinate is defined. The Scientist who buys margin walks from the Bayes pole to the salience pole and stops there.

   The 19-item spike at a normalised price of exactly 1 is the extreme tail of the same phenomenon: there the robust option is the item's **lowest**-posterior option, 17 of 19 on `manmade`, at mean `fit_cost` 0.3862 against 0.1946 on the rest of the divergence set.

   **The relation to Paper 1, stated narrowly.** Paper 1's finding is about where language models sit relative to the salience-Bayes interval. This is about where the normative optimum moves inside it, on the same coordinate, with no model involved. The two are adjacent, not the same claim, and nothing in this report measures a model.

3. **The price of robustness is 0.3056 of the item's own posterior range** on P1's `post_norm`, the primary DV (Step 3).

4. **Existence and magnitude come apart across tiles.** The per-tile existence rate spans a factor of 8.6; the median budget needed once an item diverges spans 1.22 (Step 4, exploratory).

## Step 3. The price of robustness

`L(h*|o*_0) - L(h*|o*_infinity)`: the posterior mass on the truth the Scientist gives up to buy margin against an informed adversary. Computed over the divergence set of the frozen 1,000, 460 items.

**Bug check first (v2.0 H-A).** Negative values: **0**. Non-negativity follows from `o*_0` being the posterior argmax, so any negative value would be an implementation bug rather than a finding; the script raises instead of reporting one. Exactly zero on 0 items, of which 0 carry P1's `boundary_exact` flag (2 `boundary_exact` items are in the divergence set at all).

The raw difference, which is the quantity the specification defines:

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Mean 0.0034, sd 0.0033.

### Against what reference

A raw difference of posterior masses is not interpretable on its own, and the reference is not this session's to invent. P1 already normalises this exact axis, twice, and both of its conventions are used here. Every denominator is emitted to `results/T6_arm_a_numbers.json`; none is read off prose.

The item posterior is over `|H|` = 100 hypotheses with a uniform prior, so `1/|H|` = 0.0100 of any `L(h*|o)` is a prior floor the signal did not earn. Mean `L(h*|o*_0)` over the divergence set is 0.0164, of which 0.00637 is above that floor; mean `L(h*|o*_infinity)` is 0.0130, of which 0.00300 is.

| convention | denominator | mean of per-item ratios | ratio of means | median | n | undefined |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **P1 `post_norm`, D47** (PRIMARY) | `max_o L(h*\|o) - min_o L(h*\|o)` | 0.3056 | 0.2977 | 0.2728 | 460 | 0 |
| above the prior floor | `L(h*\|o*_0) - 1/\|H\|` | 0.5779 | 0.5294 | 0.5483 | 460 | 0 |
| total posterior mass | `L(h*\|o*_0)` | 0.1819 | 0.2060 | 0.1502 | 460 | 0 |
| P1 salience-Bayes span (degenerate, below) | `1 - post_norm(o_fit)` | 1.0000 | 1.0368 | 1.0000 | 439 | 21 |

**The headline number is 0.3056**, on P1's `post_norm`. That is the right denominator for three reasons and not because of its value: it is the coordinate P1 already divides this axis by (D47, `tiebreak.norm`, whose raw range is the frozen `frontier_ext_post` column, matched here to 1.2e-16); it is P1's primary DV and the coordinate Arm B is scored on, so Arm A and Arm B stay on one scale; and it is bounded in [0, 1] with no degeneracy beyond P1's own EPS rule. Read: **the Scientist surrenders about 31% of the posterior range its own option set spans.**

The above-prior reference gives 0.5779 as a mean of per-item ratios and 0.5294 as a ratio of means. The two differ because the denominator `L(h*|o*_0) - 1/|H|` falls as low as 2.33e-05. Both are reported and neither is the headline, because `1/|H|` is not a reference P1 uses; it is the correct observation that the raw share of total mass (0.1819) counts the prior floor as earned, and it is reported for that reason.

**Not clipped:** on 42 of 460 divergent items the margin-optimal signal leaves `L(h*|o*_infinity)` at or below the prior floor, so the above-prior ratio exceeds 1 on 42 items. Those are the items where the robust choice surrenders more than the signal earned. They are kept, and they are why that column's mean of ratios sits above its ratio of means.

### P1's salience-Bayes coordinate is degenerate here, and the reason is a finding

On **452 of 460** divergent items (0.9826), `o*_infinity` is P1's `o_fit`: **the margin-maximizing signal is the salience-maximizing signal.** These are different functions and this is not an identity, since 8 exceptions exist, but all 8 of them fall inside the 21 items P1's own `span > 0.02` guard removes. So on all 439 items where the frontier coordinate is defined, `sb(o*_infinity)` is exactly zero: 439 of 439. The price is therefore 1, up to a shortfall of 5.7e-15 on the 2 items where `post_norm(o*_0)` is itself 1 only to float rounding.

Two consequences, and they point in opposite directions. As a denominator the coordinate is useless for this quantity: a constant carries no distributional information, which is why the primary is `post_norm` and not this. As a result it is the sharpest sentence in Step 3: **buying margin against an informed adversary walks the entire salience-to-Bayes interval, from the Bayes pole to the salience pole.** Paper 1's finding concerns where language models sit relative to that interval; this is a statement about where the normative optimum moves inside it, on the same coordinate. The two are adjacent, not the same claim, and nothing here measures a model.

## Step 4. What predicts a low `beta_c`. EXPLORATORY

**Exploratory throughout.** v2.0 section 10 lists "what predicts a low `beta_c` (T6 Step 4), and any regression of `beta_c` on item properties" as exploratory. There is no preregistered prediction and no threshold. No p-value below is a test.

**First, what is not a finding.** `beta_c` is finite exactly when `o*_0 != o*_infinity`. Verified on all 1000 items: `True`. Any predictor of *finiteness* that is a function of the margin gap between those two options restates the definition, so margin headroom is used below only among finite items, where its magnitude is not implied by membership.

**A float-level discrepancy, surfaced rather than hidden.** The raw sign test `headroom > 0` disagrees with finiteness on **1** of 1000 items. On those, a rival exceeds `o*_0`'s margin by less than P1's D51 tie-break tolerance, so the tie-break keeps `o*_0`, `beta_c` is infinite, and the float difference is nonetheless positive: the largest headroom among adversary-robust items is 4.4e-16, float64 noise on a log-scale difference. Nothing is clamped. The identity every other quantity in this script rests on is the argmax one, which is exact.

### Existence and magnitude come apart. EXPLORATORY

Recorded as a separate observation from the ordering check below, because it is a different and stronger statement.

| tile | pool `\|D(inf)\|/N` | median finite `beta_c`, pool | median finite `beta_c`, frozen | share of `D` above `beta = 8`, pool | frozen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `manmade` | 0.0387 | 3.0912 | 3.2385 | 0.0041 | 0.0000 |
| `moves` | 0.1790 | 3.3114 | 3.0049 | 0.0091 | 0.0000 |
| `hold` | 0.1775 | 3.7688 | 3.2589 | 0.0162 | 0.0259 |
| `size` | 0.3338 | 3.5202 | 3.6477 | 0.0194 | 0.0000 |

The existence rate spans a factor of **8.6** across the four tiles. The median budget needed, conditional on divergence, spans a factor of **1.22** on the pool and **1.21** on the frozen set. **The tiles differ in whether items diverge and barely in how much budget is needed once they do.**

This is a cleaner mechanism statement than either ordering below. It separates the existence condition from the magnitude: whatever distinguishes the tiles acts on whether `o*_0` is already the margin-maximizer, not on how far apart the two options sit in `beta`. And unlike a tile-level covariate fitted to four points, it is a claim about the shape of the `beta_c` distribution inside each tile, so it does not rest on ordering four numbers.

It also carries a reporting consequence. The grid endpoint of 8 is adequate **per tile**, not only on average: the largest per-tile share of a divergence set sitting above it is 0.0194 on the pool. A grid adequate on a pooled average could still be short on one tile, and this rules that out without extending the grid.


### The mechanism question `|O|` failed

`|O|` does not order the divergence rates. On the pool `manmade` gives 0.0387 and `moves` 0.1790 at identical `|O| = 3`, a within-`|O|=3` spread of 0.1403 against a 3-to-4 step of 0.0015 (within exceeds between: True). v2.1 section 2.2 named a candidate: each tile's rating spread relative to its bin width. That is measurable on P1's frozen fit matrices with no new data. A tile whose 1,118 concepts collapse onto few distinct fit vectors offers the adversary fewer distinguishable options than its `|O|` advertises.

**The outcome variable here is the pool rate, and this is the one place in Steps 2 through 5 where that is so.** It is not a base change. The between-tile differences the question is about were measured on the pool in Step 1, and the frozen set's per-tile rates span only 0.4320 to 0.4880 because P1 drew 250 items per tile under a 50% conflict quota on each. Correlating a structural covariate against those would read out P1's quota rather than the signal space. Both columns are shown.

| tile | `\|O\|` | concepts | distinct fit vectors | distinct frac | mean fit range | pool `\|D(inf)\|/N` | frozen finite rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `manmade` | 3 | 1118 | 400 | 0.3578 | 0.8233 | 0.0387 | 0.4560 |
| `moves` | 3 | 1118 | 580 | 0.5188 | 0.7358 | 0.1790 | 0.4880 |
| `hold` | 4 | 1118 | 609 | 0.5447 | 0.8726 | 0.1775 | 0.4640 |
| `size` | 6 | 1118 | 1104 | 0.9875 | 0.9308 | 0.3338 | 0.4320 |

Ordering by pool rate: `manmade` < `hold` < `moves` < `size`. By `|O|`: `manmade` < `moves` < `hold` < `size` (reproduces the rate order: **False**). By distinct-fit-vector fraction: `manmade` < `moves` < `hold` < `size` (reproduces it: **False**).

Neither reproduces the order exactly, and the two failures are not alike. `|O|` assigns `manmade` and `moves` the same value while their rates differ by a factor of 4.6, which is the failure that prompted the question. The distinct-fraction ordering misses only the `moves` / `hold` pair, whose rates differ by 0.0015 and are for practical purposes tied; it does separate `manmade` from the other three, which is where the large gap actually is. That is a weaker claim than "the mechanism is bin resolution" and it is the only one the four points support.

Four tiles. This is an ordering check, not evidence: with four points a rank statistic takes five values and separates nothing, and `distinct_fraction` is a tile-level constant, so it cannot be told apart from any other tile-level covariate on this design. It is reported because it is the first candidate that reproduces the ordering `|O|` fails to, and because it is falsifiable on a signal space with more tiles. The item-level version of the same question is the tile dummies in the OLS above, where `tile_size` is the largest of the three and still among the weakest terms in the model.
