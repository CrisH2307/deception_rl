## Why this was run

T6 Step 3 measured that `o*_infinity` is P1's `o_fit` on 452 of 460 divergent items. Arm B's target is therefore ON P1's salience pole. Paper 1's headline is that models sit outside the salience-Bayes interval on the far side of salience. If they already sit at or past `A = 1` under F0, before any adversary is mentioned, then Arm B's preregistered "little or no movement" prediction can be confirmed by a ceiling rather than by insensitivity.

Two consequences, and only one of them is a threat.

- **The LEVEL of `A` is confounded.** Sitting at `o*_infinity` is not evidence of adversary tracking; it is the salience-driven behaviour Paper 1 documented.
- **The CONTRASTS are not.** Salience-driven behaviour is constant across framings, so it cancels in `F1 - F0` and `F2 - F0` and yields `ΔA = 0`. Arm B's primary measure is already a contrast (v2.0 section 4), so the design survives this part. **What is at risk is power, not validity.**

## The salience reference on the `A` axis

`A_i(o*_infinity) = 1` by construction, so on every item where `o_fit` and `o*_infinity` coincide a purely salience-driven chooser scores exactly 1. That is **452 of 460** items (0.9826). Median `A_i(o_fit)` is 1.0000, range [0.0000, 1.0000].

### Implementation hazard, flagged for T7

v2.0 section 3.2 defines `A`'s domain by "`ext_i > 0` if and only if `beta_c(i) < infinity`". That equivalence holds mathematically. In float it does not, on **1** item(s) of this frozen set (ids [24130]): a rival beats `o*_0`'s margin by less than P1's D51 tie-break tolerance, so the tie-break keeps `o*_0` and `beta_c` is infinite, while `1 - marg_norm(o*_0)` comes out at 2.2e-16 rather than 0.

An implementation that takes `A`'s domain from the sign of `ext_i` divides by that number and hands Arm B a finite `A` of order 1e16 for an adversary-robust item. **This is not caught by section 6's `ext >= 0.02` floor if the floor is applied after `A` is formed**, and it is not caught by any range check that admits negative `A`. This script takes the domain from `beta_c`, which is the definition. The smallest genuine `ext_i` on the divergence set is 2.09e-04, four orders of magnitude clear of it, so the two cases are cleanly separable.

`ext_i` falls below v2.0 section 6's floor of 0.02 on 14 divergent items: `manmade` 2, `moves` 2, `hold` 3, `size` 7 Reported; the floor is not applied to the tables below, which use the full divergence set, so the two effects stay separable.

### What the two coordinates say together

They look contradictory and are not. About half of each model's choices sit **at or past the salience pole on P1's posterior coordinate**, which reproduces Paper 1's headline under F0. Almost none of that is *at* the pole: the `sb = 0` column is 0.0000 to 0.0364, so the mass is strictly on the far side. A far-side option has a **lower** posterior than `o_fit`, and `A` is the **margin** axis, on which such an option is nowhere near the maximum. Hence low `A` and negative median `A` alongside a high `sb <= 0` share. The two coordinates order options differently, and the models are past salience on one while far below the target on the other.

**The ceiling concern does not materialise.** On the axis Arm B actually scores, every model has room: `A >= 1` on 0.0174 to 0.1870 of the divergence set, median `A` at or below 0 for all seven, and median headroom between 1.0 and 1.9. A null on `ΔA` would not be produced by saturation, because there is no saturation to produce it. That is a measurement, and what follows from it is T5's to decide.
